# Social Captions

**When this skill triggers, start your response with:** `Using social-captions skill`

## Overview

Write social captions that sound like a real person, not a brand account. The goal is copy that makes someone stop scrolling — specific, honest, a little unexpected.

## Pre-Draft Protocol

Before writing:
1. What platform? (Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn — each has a different register)
2. What's the image or subject? (The caption should earn the photo, not just describe it)
3. What's the user's natural voice? (Check how they write in chat — match that energy)
4. What do they want the reader to feel or do?

If they shared a photo description, use it. If not, ask what's in it or what the moment was.

## Platform Register

**Instagram / Facebook (personal):**
- Conversational, specific, a little dry or wry
- First line is the hook — visible before "more" — make it earn the click
- A story beat or a single sharp observation beats generic enthusiasm
- Hashtags: if at all, at the end and only the relevant ones. Not a keyword dump.

**LinkedIn:**
- Still conversational, but pulled slightly toward "professional person being real"
- Lessons and takes land here if they're grounded in a specific thing that happened
- Avoid: "Excited to announce," "Thrilled to share," "Humbled by," "Game-changer"
- Lead with the thing, not with how you feel about the thing

**Org/brand account (posting as a team or organization):**
- Open with the organization or team as subject, not "I" or "we" alone
- Technical detail for audiences that came for it. Don't strip the specificity.
- Warm pivot before any PSA or call to action
- Single smiley or soft closer is okay when softening a message

## Voice Principles for Captions

**Specific beats generic.** "The third lap at elevation when your legs forget they agreed to this" beats "feeling the burn." Name the actual thing.

**Honest beats performed.** If it was hard, say it was hard. If it was beautiful, don't say "breathtaking." Say what made it beautiful.

**First line is the whole game.** Everything visible before the fold has to hold someone. Lead with the tension, the image, the joke, or the observation. Not the context.

**One idea per caption.** If there are two things to say, pick the better one. Two ideas fight each other.

**Show, don't summarize.** "The kind of morning where the coffee tastes better than usual" beats "great morning."

**End on something.** A quiet observation, a question, a single punchy line. Not "that's it for now" or "stay tuned."

## What to Avoid

- Generic enthusiasm: "So excited!" "Feeling blessed!" "Can't believe this!"
- Motivational-poster closes: "Keep going." "Dream big." "Trust the process."
- Hashtag walls unrelated to the actual content
- Listing everything in the photo instead of picking one angle
- Captions that describe exactly what anyone can see in the image

## Drafting

Present one primary draft and optionally a tighter alternate. Don't present three versions and make the user pick. Lead with your recommendation. If you're unsure which angle lands, say so and pick one anyway.

After presenting: wait for feedback. Caption edits are the best voice calibration. If they cut the last line, cut it every time. If they add warmth to a dry take, they want more warmth.

## Interaction with Other Skills

- **drafting-voice:** For non-caption copy (emails, messages, team posts), use drafting-voice instead. Social-captions is specifically for post copy.
- **tldr:** If the user has a long piece of content they want to distill into a caption-length post, run tldr first, then caption.
