noteOS Starter · Mac and Windows

An AI thinking partner that learns and grows with you.

One ever-changing folder of notes that supercharges how you work with AI. The more you chat, the more it learns. The more it learns, the more useful it gets and the more it can do for you.

This guide is written for Claude. Other AI apps that can work in a folder on your computer, like ChatGPT's desktop app, should work too, since the notes and instructions are plain text files. Start the same way: Read CLAUDE.md in this folder and follow it. (CLAUDE.md is the rulebook file inside your noteOS folder.) A guide for other apps is in the works.

Why I built this

I started reading about second brain ideas and wanted AI to grow with my day to day and help me get through work and personal things. What frustrated me was how limited AI like ChatGPT and Claude were. They only knew what was in the app and the chat or session you were in, so they didn't really grow with you.

I don't know about you, but my brain is great at coming up with ideas and not so great at storing and organizing them so I can come back to them later. And it's not only ideas. It's the meeting notes, the to-dos, the follow-ups, the tip a friend gave you, the hundred little decisions we make every day. A second brain is where all of that goes, in notes you can find and build on whenever you need them. Over time it becomes a foundational knowledge base of you: your own personal Wikipedia, or your own personal operating system built on simple notes. That's where the name noteOS comes from. Paired with AI, those notes become something you can talk to. Claude reads them, adds to them and connects them, so your second brain keeps growing every time you use it. And it doesn't stop at notes. Once it knows your work, you can start building things with it: plans, scripts, websites, tools, whatever you can think of. Since it already has the context, what you build together comes out better.

Then I gave Claude a folder of my notes. It started referencing other things it had created and giving me context on other things I was doing. From there I built out the whole basecamp framework.

I'm sharing it because I feel like people still aren't getting the most out of their AI, especially when it comes to connecting dots. I want you to have an ever-growing knowledge base and a true, consistent thinking partner on your computer that has the context of what you're doing, so you don't feel like you're starting over with every new chat window. And since it's all simple notes, folders and files on your computer, it's pretty easy for anyone to get set up.

It remembers what you don't

Imagine a notebook that remembers everything you've told it, and talks back.

  • You close your laptop in the middle of a project on Friday. Monday morning you say "pick up where we left off," and it does. No re-explaining.
  • A contractor gave you a quote three months ago. You ask about it, and it finds the number, what you decided and why.
  • You start sketching a new idea, and it reminds you of something you jotted down last spring that fits right in.

And it builds itself as you go. You talk, it takes the notes and links them together. No folders to organize first.

What this is

Your noteOS folder is a folder of notes, and Claude lives in that folder. Every conversation can leave notes behind. Those notes are plain text files you can open and read. Over time they become Claude's knowledge base about your work: your projects, what you did last week, where you left off. Claude reads that, it's caught up, and you keep going. That's what I call my second brain.

Think of your noteOS folder as the hub for everything you do with your AI. It starts as notes, folders and markdown files, but it can become so much more. noteOS Starter is a template: a jumping off point that holds your hand and gets you going, so you can build and grow your own version of this organically with your AI thinking partner. Before long you'll be building things with it, and having it help with things you never even thought of right now.

It's the start of a thinking partner, a business partner and a creative partner that gets more useful the more you use it. Nobody sends you updates. It changes through your own use, and when Claude wants to change one of its own rules, it asks you first.

Five ideas that make it work

These are the ideas that make the biggest difference. Your noteOS folder has all five built in.

1

It asks before it builds

Before Claude makes anything you'll keep, like a plan, a document or a script, it asks you questions, one at a time, each one building on your last answer. Then it writes from your answers, in your words, so the result sounds like you. If it fills a gap with its own guess, it tells you.

2

It remembers

Every conversation can leave notes behind, and Claude reads them next time, so it doesn't start from scratch. Some of it happens as you go: Claude logs routine things in today's note and asks before saving bigger decisions. The rest happens when you end a session by saying "save this session." Behind the scenes it pulls out the decisions, new facts and next steps, files each one in the right note, links it to the right basecamp and updates Current Context with where you stopped. The next morning, say "pick up where we left off" and it tells you where you stopped. (In Cowork, if Claude doesn't seem to know you, send Read CLAUDE.md in this folder and follow it. first. In the Code tab, /remember and /pickup work too.)

Want to see the notes? Open the folder in Obsidian, a free app that shows every note Claude has made for you in an easy-to-read format. Or skip it entirely. Keep talking to Claude and ask it for whatever you need, and you never have to open a note yourself. That's the beauty of it.

3

Basecamps connect the dots

On a big climb, basecamp is home base. It's where you keep your gear, plan the route and come back to between pushes. A noteOS basecamp works the same way for one part of your life, like a project, a client, a hobby or a trip. It's one note that links to every note about that topic. At the top is the Compass, a short summary that gets you oriented: what this is and where it connects. At the bottom are the Trails, the links out to everything under it. Claude starts at the basecamp, gets its bearings from the Compass, then follows the Trails, so it finds the right context fast. Onboarding makes your first basecamps from what you tell it about your life.

4

It learns and grows with you

When you correct Claude, it writes it down in the Lessons Log. Say you tell it "that's wrong, I posted that on Tuesday, not Monday." It logs the mistake. If the same kind of mistake happens again, it suggests a rule, like "check the daily note before saying when something happened," and adds it to CLAUDE.md or the skill involved once you say OK. When you rewrite one of its drafts, it learns your voice the same way. Run a tune-up about once a week and it suggests ways to make its skills better.

5

It has playbooks

Skills are saved ways of doing things, like setting up, planning or wrapping up a session. Your noteOS folder starts you off with a playbook of 12, from onboarding and the guided tour to remember, pickup and plan. See the full noteOS Starter Playbook below. When you find yourself doing the same thing more than once, Claude can turn it into a new skill, so next time it's one request. The plan skill thinks a project through before anything gets built, and in the Code tab you can switch to a cheaper model for the building.

A nice side effect: your plan goes further

If you keep hitting your Claude usage limit, this part helps. Think of Claude's models as a team. Opus is the architect: the stronger model, best at thinking a problem through and writing the plan. Sonnet is the construction crew: it costs less, and it does great work once it has a good plan to follow.

The plan skill always writes the plan first, so you can have Opus plan and Sonnet build. How fast you reach a limit depends on the model you use, so letting the cheaper model do most of the work usually makes your usage go further. In the Code tab it takes a few clicks, and the setup guide shows you how.

How the folder works

Everything in your noteOS folder is a plain text file on your computer. You can edit or delete any of it, and Claude reads the new version next session.

You talk, Claude writes. The folder grows from your conversations. Claude writes the daily note, makes your first basecamps, suggests more and links each new note to the right one, so you rarely need to make, name or move a file yourself. The one exception is Downloads/, where you drop files you want Claude to read. Map, Calendar, Downloads and Templates each have an About note inside that says what goes in it.

Markdown notes

A markdown file is a plain text file whose name ends in .md. Obsidian shows it as a tidy note. The files are tiny, and any text editor can open them.

CLAUDE.md

The instruction file. It has an About Me section that stays empty until your first conversation, plus the habits Claude follows on every message, like searching your notes before it answers, asking before large changes and saying how sure it is.

Basecamps

One home base note per topic, with the Compass at the top and the Trails at the bottom. When Claude makes a new note, it adds it to the right basecamp's Trails. The five ideas above explain how basecamps work.

Skills

A skill is a saved playbook. Each one tells Claude how to do one job the same way every time, like saving a session or drafting an email. Ask for one by name, or describe the job in plain words. When you've done something twice the same way, Claude can turn it into a new skill.

Logs

When you correct Claude, it adds a row to the Lessons Log. If the same kind of mistake shows up a second time, Claude proposes a fixed rule and waits for your yes. The Voice Log tracks how you edit its drafts, and a pattern that shows up three times becomes part of your voice.

Current Context

The whiteboard note. It holds what's active and where things left off. Claude reads it at the start of a session, and the remember skill updates it at the end.

The noteOS Starter Playbook: 12 skills to get you headed in the right direction

  • onboarding Setup on your first conversation. Asks about you, your apps and your writing, then fills in About Me.
  • tour A short guided tour after setup: markdown notes, your CLAUDE.md, basecamps, skills and the logs, one idea at a time.
  • interview Asks one question at a time, then writes from your answers.
  • plan Writes a plan first, then does the work after you approve it.
  • remember Saves the session to the daily note and updates Current Context.
  • pickup Reads Current Context and tells you where you left off.
  • make-a-skill Turns something you've done twice into a new skill.
  • voice Drafts messages in your own style and learns from your edits.
  • learn Logs a correction and proposes a rule on the second strike.
  • tune-up A weekly check that proposes skill edits for you to approve.
  • chief-of-staff Reads your notes and connected apps, then tells you what needs you.
  • connect Explains connections and sets up an app for Claude to use.

Day 1 in five steps

Before you start, install Obsidian from obsidian.md/download and the Claude desktop app from claude.com/download, then sign in to Claude. The setup guides walk through both installs click by click.

  1. Move the folder to Documents

    Unzip noteOS.zip with the default settings. On a Mac, double-click it. On Windows, right-click it, choose Extract All, keep the location it suggests (don't click Browse, or the files land loose), and click Extract. You get a folder named noteOS. Drag that folder into your Documents folder. You can rename it, for example to Notes. This folder is Claude's home now, and Downloads tends to get cleaned out. Then open it in Obsidian with Open folder as vault.

  2. Open Cowork

    Open the Claude desktop app and click Cowork. Depending on your app version, it's a tab at the top of the sidebar on the left or an option near the box you type in.

  3. Pick your folder

    Choose your notes folder in Documents. Look for a folder control near the box. If you don't see one, go on to the next step. Cowork asks you to pick a folder the first time it needs files.

  4. Send the first message

    Type this exactly:

    Read CLAUDE.md in this folder and follow it.

    Cowork doesn't read CLAUDE.md on its own. When I tried hi, I got a generic reply. This sentence points Claude at your instruction file.

  5. Click Allow on each prompt

    A few permission prompts come next. These are the ones I saw on my Mac, in this order. The prompt shows your folder's name, so if you renamed your noteOS folder, you see that name instead of "noteOS."

    1. Claude asks

      Allow Claude to change files in "noteOS"?

      Click Always allow, since Claude writes notes here. Do this for your notes folder only, never for a folder of originals you can't replace.

    2. Claude asks

      Allow Claude to access this folder?

      It says the session runs in the cloud and the files Claude uses are processed on Anthropic's servers. Check Don't ask again for this folder on this device, then click Allow.

    3. macOS asks

      "Claude" would like to access files in your Documents folder.

      Click Allow. Mine named Downloads, because my test folder was still there. With your folder in Documents, it should name Documents.

    On Windows, I haven't seen these prompts yet. The Windows guide shows the Mac wording for the two Claude prompts, so yours may look a little different. If a prompt asks about Claude and your notes folder, Allow is the right answer.

Then onboarding starts. Claude welcomes you and asks about six questions, one at a time, about you, your work and how you like answers. Short answers are fine. When it's done, About Me in CLAUDE.md should be filled in and Map should hold a basecamp for each area you named.

Near the end, Claude asks to save a note in the folder's instructions so it reads CLAUDE.md first every time. Say yes. That part is new and hasn't been tested yet. If it doesn't stick, nothing breaks. Start each session with the same first message.

After Day 1, one habit at a time

You don't need all of this on day one. Each day after that, you try one new habit in a few minutes.

  1. Day 2

    Remember and pickup

    At the end of a session, type save this session. The next morning, type pick up where we left off.

  2. Day 3

    Interview and voice

    Ask for something you'd write anyway, answer the questions, then change one line and paste it back so Claude learns how you write.

  3. Day 4

    Plan

    Type let's plan and describe something with a few parts. Claude interviews you, shows a plan, and builds after you say yes.

  4. Day 5

    Connect and chief-of-staff

    Type connect my calendar. You sign in to the app yourself, in your browser. Then type where do things stand for a short read on your day.

  5. Week 2

    Learn, tune-up and make-a-skill

    Correct Claude once and watch the Lessons Log. Run tune up once a week. Turn something you've done twice into your first skill.
